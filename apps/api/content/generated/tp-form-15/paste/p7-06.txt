Review — Iron Pot Kitchen, District 4 (4 stars)

The first thing you notice at Iron Pot is the sound: six open stations, one wok roaring, and a chalkboard that changes every Thursday when the market prices are pinned up. Lunch for office crowds runs thirty-five to fifty thousand dong, generous enough that two colleagues report sharing one clay pot and leaving full.

Service is where the room earns its stars. Refills arrive before you ask, and when a batch of rice came out under-seasoned, the kitchen replaced the pot at the same table without the waiter being prompted. Manager-level staff eat in the dining room with everyone else, which regulars say keeps both the portions and the temper honest.

The catch is the timing: peak at noon means a queue behind the door from twelve-fifteen, and the two vegetarian dishes rotate out by twelve-thirty most days. Go early, or book the counter stools online, and Iron Pot is the rare canteen that tastes like someone's home kitchen at lunch rush prices.
