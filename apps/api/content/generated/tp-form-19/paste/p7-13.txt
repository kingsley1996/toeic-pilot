City Arts Daily, December 2 — Veteran Potter Pham Thi Hoa Marks 40 Years with Clay
Pham Thi Hoa, 62, has shaped celadon pottery in Bat Trang village for four decades using natural river clay and wood-fired kilns. She trains twelve young apprentices free of charge every summer and refuses to use chemical glaze to keep the traditional blue-green color. All works in the museum shop are made in Hoa's workshop in Bat Trang, according to the cultural center. Her anniversary exhibition at the City Art Museum will run from December 12 to December 20, showing sixty large vases alongside small cups for daily use. Tickets are $8, with morning visits recommended to avoid crowds.
[PASSAGE]
Exhibition Review: Quiet Beauty in Blue-Green, by Critic Le Mai, December 14
The anniversary show gathers sixty pieces, and the blue-white vases on the second floor are the most striking. The morning light brings out the depth of the blue glaze, so visitors should arrive before 10 A.M. to see the works at their best. Two small bowls near the entrance show tiny cracks from firing, but they do not reduce the overall charm. The gift corner sells miniature cups at $15 each, ideal for office desks. The catalog lacks English explanations, which may disappoint foreign guests, but the staff offers free guided tours at 2 P.M.
