Review: The Last Ferry Crosses Slowly, Lands Softly

By Dana Kim

Director Jonah Price's two-hour-ten-minute drama follows a retired captain forced to sell his boat. [1] The harbour photography is stunning, with dawn scenes that carry whole minutes without dialogue. [2] Lead actor Marco Ruiz gives a quiet, convincing performance as a man losing his livelihood. [3] Only the final twenty minutes disappoint, rushing a farewell the story had earned slowly. [4]

In my opinion, the ferry scenes alone are worth the ticket. The director grew up in this harbour town. Tickets cost twelve dollars, and evening shows sold out through the weekend. A morning screening has been added on Sunday. Price previously directed two short documentaries about river life. The script took four years to finish, with three different endings. Teenagers under thirteen must come with an adult.
